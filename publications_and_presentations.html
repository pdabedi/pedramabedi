LEGGOS II: A Strong Lens Model and Source-Plane Projection of the Clumpy Star-Forming Galaxy SGAS J111020.0+645950.8 at z = 2.481

Pedram Abedi, Keren Sharon, Taylor A. Hutchison, et al.

Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing by galaxy clusters combined with the resolution of JWST enables studies of star formation on ∼10–100 pc scales in galaxies at z ∼ 2–4. As part of the LEnsing and Galaxy Growth: Observing Substructures survey (LEGGOS), we present an updated strong lensing model of the galaxy cluster SDSS J1110+6459 (z = 0.659), which lenses the clumpy star-forming galaxy SGAS J111020.0+645950.8 at z = 2.481 into a highly magnified giant arc. Using JWST NIRCam imaging, NIRSpec spectroscopy, and archival HST data, we confirm and refine the identification of four multiply imaged background sources, including one newly identified system, and map over 20 luminous regions between each image of the primary arc. Spectroscopy confirms that several previously ambiguous edge “clumps” belong to the main arc at z = 2.481. Despite the limited number of strongly lensed sources in the field, the resulting lens model has high precision, owing to the high density of JWST-resolved clump constraints that tightly probe the lensing potential near the giant arc. The model yields a projected lens mass of M(<250 kpc) = 1.21+0.09−0.04 × 1014 M⊙, an Einstein radius of θE = 10.8+0.3−0.4 arcsec, and a total effective areal magnification of μtot = 24.2+3.4−1.2 for the giant arc. Across the arc, individual clump magnifications span μclump ∼ 4–19, with fractional magnification uncertainties of σμ/|μbest| ∼ 0.03–0.09. We report a ∼2.6–8.3× improvement in magnification precision over previous models, and estimate a systematic uncertainty of ∼7%. Ongoing and future analyses of this arc will enable robust measurements of star-forming structures, building on the lensing foundation established here for LEGGOS studies of galaxy growth and feedback during cosmic noon.

SN Helios: A Multiply Imaged Type II Supernova Opening Time-Delay Cosmography beyond redshift of 3

Seiji Fujimoto, Conor Larison, Justin D. R. Pierel, et al.

Summary

We report the discovery and spectroscopic confirmation of SN Helios, a gravitationally lensed Type II supernova at z = 3.34 behind the galaxy cluster RXC J0018.5+1626. Independent cluster lens models predict another image appearing approximately two to six years later, offering an opportunity for a future time-delay measurement of the Hubble constant using a source beyond redshift three.

Submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics · 2026

LEGGOS: Direct abundances of N, O, Ne, S, and Ar in five lensed galaxies at Cosmic Noon

Brian Welch, Gourav Khullar, Taylor A. Hutchison, et al.

Summary

Using JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy, we measure chemical abundances in five gravitationally lensed galaxies at cosmic noon. Electron-temperature measurements provide direct constraints on their gas-phase composition, allowing us to investigate how recent star formation and supernova enrichment shape the chemical properties of distant galaxies.

LEGGOS I: The JWST LEGGOS Survey—LEnsing and Galaxy Growth: Observing Substructures—Unpacks the Nature of Clumpy Star Formation and Quenching in Gravitationally Lensed Galaxies beyond Cosmic Noon

Gourav Khullar, Michael Florian, Matthew B. Bayliss, et al.

Summary

This survey overview introduces JWST LEGGOS, which combines strong gravitational lensing, multiband imaging, and spatially resolved spectroscopy to study eight distant galaxies. The survey investigates stellar populations, star formation, quenching, and interstellar gas within individual clumps and their surrounding diffuse regions, revealing complexity that imaging alone cannot fully distinguish.

LEGGOS III: Mapping Star Formation and Dust in Gravitationally Lensed Galaxies with SUMAC, a UMAP and Clustering Framework

Alex Ross, Gourav Khullar, Taylor Hutchison, et al.

Summary

We introduce SUMAC, an unsupervised machine-learning framework for identifying regions with similar photometric or spectral properties in JWST observations. Applied to the lensed galaxy SGAS J1110 at z = 2.481, the method distinguishes stellar and nebular populations, including regions with different levels of dust obscuration and star formation.

Magnifying Clumpy Star Formation at Cosmic Noon with JWST and Strong Lensing

Pedram Abedi, Keren Sharon, JWST LEGGOS Collaboration

Michigan Astronomy Undergraduate Research Symposium

Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing combined with JWST resolution enables studies of star formation on ∼10–100 pc scales at cosmic noon. As part of the JWST LEGGOS survey, we present an updated strong lens model of the galaxy cluster SDSS J1110+6459 (z = 0.659), which lenses the clumpy star-forming galaxy SGAS J1110 at z = 2.481 into a highly magnified ∼17″ giant arc. Using JWST NIRCam imaging, NIRSpec spectroscopy, and archival HST data, we confirm and refine four multiply imaged background sources and map >20 star-forming clumps across each image of the primary arc. The model yields a total magnification of ∼24 for the giant arc, with clump magnifications spanning ∼4–19. Fractional magnification uncertainties across the arc range between 3–9%, with a ∼2–8× improvement in clump magnification precision over previous work. This precision is enabled by the high density of JWST-resolved clump constraints, which tightly probe the underlying mass distribution. These results provide a foundation for resolving star-forming structure and studying galaxy growth at cosmic noon.

Poster · 2026

An Unusual Stellar System in the Background of Messier 83

Pedram Abedi, Eric Bell

Michigan Astronomy Undergraduate Research Symposium

Abstract as presented

Faint stellar systems probe the low-mass end of galaxy formation and the boundary between star clusters and dwarf galaxies. We present the discovery and analysis of GHOSTS II, an unusual stellar system identified in archival HST imaging from the GHOSTS survey toward Messier 83 (M83). Although projected near M83, its stellar population indicates a distance of ∼7.6 Mpc, showing that it is a background system and not physically associated with the galaxy. Using masked imaging and aperture photometry, we measure its structural and integrated properties. The system has a compact half-light radius of 31–33 pc, an absolute magnitude of M ∼ −7, a color of ∼0.46 mag, and appears isolated with no nearby host galaxy within ∼1 Mpc. These properties place it in an unusual regime: compact and low-luminosity like an extended cluster, yet isolated and exhibiting a color suggestive of a younger, possibly star-forming population. GHOSTS II does not match known classes of stellar systems, and its properties provide insight into formation pathways, structural evolution, and dark matter at the lowest mass scales.

Subsequent analysis revised the interpretation of this system to a stellar population associated with M83’s halo.

Poster · 2025

Exploring the Outskirts of M81: Detection and Classification of Companions

Pedram M. Abedi, Eric F. Bell

Michigan Astronomy Undergraduate Research Symposium

Abstract

The ΛCDM model predicts more faint stellar companions than we observe, a discrepancy known as the Missing Satellites Problem. M81, a nearby spiral galaxy at ∼3.61 Mpc, provides an opportunity to search for ultra-faint stellar systems beyond the Local Group. Using high-resolution HST data from the GHOSTS survey (de Jong et al. 2006), we identified two young stellar systems in Field 8. Their compact size, morphology, and color suggest they may be previously unknown clusters or extremely faint stellar populations in M81’s outer halo.
