Using gravitational lensing to resolve the structure of distant galaxies.

A lens model for SDSS J1110+6459

Massive galaxy clusters bend the light from galaxies behind them, producing magnified and distorted images. By modeling the cluster’s mass distribution, we can map these images back to the original galaxy and estimate its intrinsic size and brightness.

As part of the LEGGOS survey, I developed an updated strong lens model of SDSS J1110+6459, a galaxy cluster at redshift z = 0.659. It lenses the star-forming galaxy SGAS J1110, at z = 2.481, into three images that form a giant arc.

What I did

I combined JWST NIRCam imaging, NIRSpec spectroscopy, and archival Hubble observations to identify four multiply imaged background sources and match more than 20 luminous regions across the primary arc. These resolved structures provide a dense set of constraints on the cluster’s lensing potential.

Key results

  • The model reproduces observed image positions with an image-plane RMS of approximately 0.11 arcseconds.
  • The giant arc has a total effective areal magnification of 24.2+3.4 −1.2 , allowing us to infer the source’s intrinsic area.
  • Magnification precision improves by approximately 2.6–8.3 times over previous models, with an estimated systematic uncertainty of about 10%.
  • The three independently reconstructed images recover consistent source morphology, including bright star-forming regions.

Why it matters

A well-constrained lens model is the foundation for measuring the sizes, luminosities, and spatial distribution of star-forming structures in this galaxy. This work supplies the magnification maps and source-plane projections needed for further LEGGOS studies of galaxy growth and feedback.
